Read off the drawing

The overwing is modelled as the crescent on your top view: a cuff that wraps the main wing's leading edge and widens into the outer tip panel. Wing setting angle (2°) and airfoil (NACA 2412) are my assumptions; the drawing does not give them.

Start from

Wing

Sliding the wing aft puts the lift behind the CG, which is what makes it stable. A wider tip and washout stop the tips stalling first.

Tail

A bigger tail lifts the nose earlier on take-off and adds a little stability.

Landing gear

Main wheels belong just behind the CG, with 8 to 15% of the weight on the nose wheel. A wider track resists tipping.

Load

Nose ballast moves the CG forward, but every kilo raises the stall speed.

Wind tunnel

Streamlines are coloured by air speed and the wing by how much lift each part carries. Tufts lie flat in attached flow and flail red where it has separated. This is potential-flow theory, not a full CFD solver: it shows no turbulent wakes.

Mount

Held: you set the angles. Free: the model pivots at its CG and only the control surfaces steer it, which shows whether it is stable.

Lift curve

Pitching moment about the CG

A line that falls to the right means the nose comes back down by itself. Rising means unstable.
